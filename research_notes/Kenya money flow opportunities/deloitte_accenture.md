## 1. Global AI spending intentions: who spends, how much, and what blocks scaling (Deloitte)

### Takeaway
Large enterprises keep raising AI budgets (78% of respondents expect to increase AI spend). Scaling is held back by **regulation/risk, data management, and governance**, not by lack of money. The spend is moving toward data foundations, cybersecurity, and governance/agent oversight, and those are the second-order services opportunities.

### Cited Findings
- **[S] AI spend up.** "A vast majority of respondents (78%) expect to increase their overall AI spending in the next fiscal year." Deloitte, *State of Generative AI in the Enterprise, Q4 report* (published Jan 2025; survey of 2,773 director-to-C-suite respondents across 14 countries, July–Sept 2024) — [Deloitte US press release](https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/about/press-room/state-of-generative-ai.html); [Q4 PDF](https://www.deloitte.com/content/dam/assets-zone3/us/en/docs/services/consulting/2024/us-state-of-gen-ai-q4.pdf). *Commercial meaning:* the budget exists at enterprise level. The open question is who converts it into production.
- **[S] Slow scaling.** "More than two-thirds of respondents say that 30% or fewer of their experiments will be fully scaled in the next three to six months." Same Q4 report — [Deloitte Q4 PDF](https://www.deloitte.com/content/dam/assets-zone3/us/en/docs/services/consulting/2024/us-state-of-gen-ai-q4.pdf). *Meaning:* the pilot-to-production gap is a services market in implementation, integration and MLOps.
- **[S] Regulation is the top barrier.** "Regulation and risk have emerged as the top barrier to development and deployment—increasing 10 percentage points from Q1 to Q4"; concern about regulatory compliance "jumped from 28% to 38%" between survey waves. Same report — [Deloitte US press release](https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/about/press-room/state-of-generative-ai.html). *Meaning:* AI compliance and governance services are a named, growing pain point.
- **[S] Governance takes time.** "69% of organizations expect it will take at least a year to implement a comprehensive [governance] strategy." Same report — [Deloitte Q4 PDF](https://www.deloitte.com/content/dam/assets-zone3/us/en/docs/services/consulting/2024/us-state-of-gen-ai-q4.pdf).
- **[S] Data management investment.** "75% of organisations are increasing their technology investments around data management due to GenAI." Deloitte, *State of Generative AI in the Enterprise, Q3 report* (Aug 2024; 2,770 leaders directly involved in piloting or implementing GenAI, 14 countries, survey May–June 2024) — [Deloitte US press release, Q3](https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/about/press-room/state-of-generative-ai-Q3.html); related: [Deloitte Insights, "Gen AI investment opportunities center on data, cybersecurity, and cloud"](https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/insights/topics/emerging-technologies/ai-investment-opportunities-tech-ecosystem.html). *Meaning:* data readiness (data cleaning, cataloguing, data lifecycle management) is where GenAI budgets flow first.
- **[S/F] Agents are coming, but governance lags.** In Deloitte's *State of AI in the Enterprise 2026* (survey of 3,235 leaders, Aug–Sept 2025; publication date around early 2026, not confirmed): "by 2027, 74% of respondents expect their companies to be using AI agents at least 'moderately'", with 23% expecting "extensively". "85% of companies are expecting to customize agents." "Only 21% of those companies report having a mature model for agent governance." — [Deloitte US, State of AI in the Enterprise 2026](https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/what-we-do/capabilities/applied-artificial-intelligence/content/state-of-ai-in-the-enterprise.html); [Deloitte press release](https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/about/press-room/state-of-ai-report-2026.html); [Deloitte Insights, "Agentic AI is scaling faster than guardrails"](https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/insights/topics/emerging-technologies/ai-agents-scaling-faster.html). *Meaning:* agent customisation (implementation) and agent governance/audit are two separate opportunities, and the 85% vs 21% gap measures the size of the governance one.

### Inferences
- Across three Deloitte survey waves the bottleneck has shifted from "is it useful?" to "can we govern, secure and feed it data?". A small firm cannot compete for the model or infrastructure spend, but it can sell data preparation, compliance documentation, and agent monitoring to mid-market firms that copy enterprise behaviour with a lag.

### Gaps
- Deloitte gives no Africa-specific breakdown in these AI surveys. Kenya/Africa are not visible in the 14-country samples (country list not verified).
- Exact page and figure numbers are unverified because fetching was blocked.

## 2. Infrastructure and compute: where the largest dollars go (Deloitte TMT Predictions and Tech Trends)

### Takeaway
The biggest money (hundreds of billions of dollars) goes into data centres and chips that hyperscalers and large enterprises capture. The second-order problem Deloitte names is **runaway inference cost and infrastructure unsuited to AI**, which opens room for cost optimisation, hybrid/colocation advisory and FinOps.

### Cited Findings
- **[F] Capex.** AI data-centre and chip capex is "expected to reach $400–450 billion globally in 2026." Deloitte, *TMT Predictions 2026* (published ~Nov 2025; date not confirmed) — [Deloitte Global press release](https://www.deloitte.com/global/en/about/press-room/2026-tmt-predictions.html); [Deloitte Insights TMT Predictions 2026](https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/insights/industry/technology/technology-media-and-telecom-predictions.html).
- **[F] Inference share of compute.** "Inference … will account for two-thirds of all AI computing power by 2026." Most inference will run "in new data centers worth nearly half a trillion dollars and in on-premises enterprise servers using costly, power-intensive AI chips worth over $200 billion, rather than at the edge." Same source — [Deloitte Insights TMT Predictions 2026](https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/insights/industry/technology/technology-media-and-telecom-predictions.html). *Meaning:* the power, cooling and colocation demand is real. In Kenya, this favours data-centre-adjacent services (facility operations, power backup, connectivity) over edge-device plays.
- **[F] Agent market size.** "The autonomous AI agent market could reach $8.5 billion by 2026 and $35 billion by 2030", rising "up to 30% — or as high as $45 billion by 2030" if enterprises orchestrate agents better. Same source — [Deloitte Global press release](https://www.deloitte.com/global/en/about/press-room/2026-tmt-predictions.html). *Meaning:* agents are a small market in dollar terms compared with infrastructure, but services-heavy.
- **[A/claim] Inference cost paradox.** Inference costs are "dropping 280-fold over the last two years", yet "some organizations are seeing massive monthly bills because usage is growing far faster than costs are falling." "Organizations are hitting a tipping point where cloud services become cost-prohibitive for high-volume workloads." Deloitte, *Tech Trends 2026*, chapter "The AI infrastructure reckoning" (published ~Dec 2025) — [Deloitte Insights](https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/insights/topics/technology-management/tech-trends/2026/ai-infrastructure-compute-strategy.html); [Tech Trends 2026 PDF](https://mkto.deloitte.com/rs/712-CNF-326/images/DI_Tech-trends-2026.pdf). *Meaning:* AI cost management (FinOps, usage monitoring, model routing) is a named unsolved problem, and one that can be delivered remotely.

### Inferences
- Deloitte's TMT view that inference will run in data centres rather than at the edge argues against pitching edge-AI hardware and in favour of cloud or colocation services and cost optimisation.

### Gaps
- No Deloitte figure on African data-centre capex was found.
- The "Deloitte TMT Predictions 2025" figures on data-centre power were not retrieved in this session.

## 3. Cybersecurity: budgets and readiness gaps (Accenture and Deloitte)

### Takeaway
Both firms report that most organisations are **not ready to secure AI**. Accenture says 90% are not adequately prepared, and Deloitte says 58% expect cyber budgets to rise. This is a large, recurring managed-services opportunity (security operations, AI/data security, security embedded in transformation projects).

### Cited Findings
- **[S] Readiness zones.** "Only 10%" of organisations are in the "Reinvention Ready Zone"; "63%" are in the "Exposed Zone"; "27%" are "Progressing." "90% of organizations are not adequately prepared to secure their AI-driven future." Accenture, *State of Cybersecurity Resilience 2025* (published ~June 2025) — [Accenture report page](https://www.accenture.com/en/insights/security/state-cybersecurity-2025); [PDF](https://www.accenture.com/content/dam/accenture/final/accenture-com/document-3/State-of-Cybersecurity-report.pdf); [Accenture newsroom, "Only One in 10 Organizations Globally Are Ready…"](https://newsroom.accenture.com/news/2025/only-one-in-10-organizations-globally-are-ready-to-protect-against-ai-augmented-cyber-threats).
- **[S] Security gaps.** "77% of organizations lack the essential data and AI security practices needed to protect critical business models, data pipelines and cloud infrastructure." "Only 34%" have a mature cyber strategy. "Just 28%" embed security into transformation initiatives from the outset. Same source — [Accenture PDF](https://www.accenture.com/content/dam/accenture/final/accenture-com/document-3/State-of-Cybersecurity-report.pdf). *Meaning:* "security by design" work on digital and AI projects is underbought. A small firm can attach security reviews to other vendors' implementations.
- **[S] Payoff of maturity.** Reinvention Ready companies are "69% less likely to face advanced attacks" and "1.5 times more effective at blocking them." Same source — [Accenture report page](https://www.accenture.com/en/insights/security/state-cybersecurity-2025).
- **[S/F] Cyber budgets rising.** "58% of respondents anticipate increasing their cyber budget over the next 12 to 24 months," and 58% "expect to begin integrating cybersecurity spending with budgets for other programs, such as digital transformation initiatives, IT programs, and cloud investments." Deloitte, *Global Future of Cyber Survey, 4th edition* (2024) — [Deloitte Global press release](https://www.deloitte.com/global/en/about/press-room/deloitte-global-future-cyber-survey.html); [Deloitte CE report page](https://www.deloitte.com/ce/en/services/consulting-risk/research/global-future-of-cyber.html). *Meaning:* cyber money is increasingly bundled into transformation budgets, so selling cyber alongside digital projects fits how buyers now budget. (A 5th edition exists — [Deloitte NZ](https://www.deloitte.com/nz/en/services/consulting-risk/research/global-future-of-cyber.html) — but its statistics were not retrieved.)
- **[Qualitative] East African banks.** "Increased digital integration heightens exposure to cyber threats, requiring banks to build robust cybersecurity cultures to maintain customer trust." The report also stresses business continuity, operational resilience, and staying agile to "anticipated regulatory changes." Deloitte, *East Africa Banking Industry Outlook Report 2026* — [Deloitte Kenya](https://www.deloitte.com/ke/en/Industries/financial-services/about/east-africa-banking-outlook-report.html). *Meaning:* Kenyan and East African banks name cyber, resilience and regulatory agility as priorities. These are buyers of security operations, continuity testing and compliance services. **No quantitative spend figure was retrieved.**

### Inferences
- The two firms agree on direction: both find most organisations under-prepared and budgets rising. They measure different things (maturity zones vs budget intent), so their numbers are not directly comparable.
- The Accenture figure that only 28% embed security from the outset suggests that "security wrap" services on other vendors' projects are a practical entry point for a small firm.

### Gaps
- No Africa-specific or Kenya-specific cyber budget figure from Deloitte or Accenture was found. Accenture published an India executive summary of the cyber report, but no Africa cut was found.
- No figure for cyber spending as a share of the IT budget was found.

## 4. Data readiness, AI scaling and workforce (Accenture)

### Takeaway
Accenture's data says the binding constraint is **AI-ready data**: only 7% of organisations have it. Executives nonetheless keep increasing GenAI investment (86%). Data engineering and data governance services therefore sit directly on the critical path.

### Cited Findings
- **[S] AI-ready data.** "Only 7% of organizations—data reinventors—have reached the level of data-readiness required to scale advanced AI" (generative, agentic, physical). "70% of the companies surveyed acknowledged the need for a strong data foundation." Data reinventors see "profit margin uplifts up to 1.6x over industry peers." Accenture, *AI-Ready Data* research and *The Front-runners' Guide to Scaling AI* (2025) — [Accenture AI-ready data](https://www.accenture.com/en/insights/ai-data/ai-ready-data); [Front-runners' Guide PDF](https://www.accenture.com/content/dam/accenture/final/accenture-com/document-3/Accenture-Front-Runners-Guide-Scaling-AI-2025-POV.pdf).
- **[S] AI readiness.** "Only 15% of companies are AI reinvention-ready." Accenture, *Front-runners' Guide* / AI research (2025) — [Accenture Front-runners' Guide page](https://www.accenture.com/my-en/insights/ai-data/front-runners-guide-scaling-ai). **Flag:** the extract did not make clear which of the two linked Accenture pages this number comes from. Verify before citing.
- **[S] GenAI investment intent.** "86% of C-suite leaders plan to increase investments in generative AI"; "90% would continue to invest in AI even if a bubble burst, yet only 10% believe a significant bubble exists." Accenture, *Pulse of Change* (2025 wave; exact wave/date not confirmed) — [Accenture Pulse of Change](https://www.accenture.com/us-en/insights/pulse-of-change). *Meaning:* demand looks durable even if sentiment turns.
- **[S] Executive views on agents.** "78% of executives believe digital ecosystems will need to be developed for AI agents just as much as for humans within the next 3–5 years"; "77% of executives believe unlocking the true benefits of AI will only be possible when it is built on a foundation of trust"; "69% of executives believe AI brings new urgency to reinvention." Accenture, *Technology Vision 2025: AI: A Declaration of Autonomy* (Jan 2025) — [Accenture newsroom](https://newsroom.accenture.com/news/2025/accenture-technology-vision-2025-new-age-of-ai-to-bring-unprecedented-autonomy-to-business); [Tech Vision 2025 PDF (investor site)](https://investor.accenture.com/~/media/Files/A/accenture-v4/investors/home/quick-links/accenture-Tech-Vision-2025.pdf). *Meaning:* "trust" (governance, security, explainability) is framed as the precondition for value, which lines up with Deloitte's governance findings.
- **[S] GenAI returns.** "93% of executives report that gen AI investments are outperforming other investments"; "97% … believe this technology will fundamentally transform their companies and industries." Accenture, *Reinvention in the Age of Generative AI* (Jan 2024) — [Accenture executive summary PDF](https://www.accenture.com/content/dam/accenture/final/accenture-com/document-2/Accenture-reinvention-in-the-age-of-generative-AI-executive-summary.pdf). **Flag:** this is self-reported executive perception, and it is more bullish than Deloitte's measured scaling rates (see Section 6).

### Inferences
- A 7% readiness rate combined with 86% investment intent is the clearest "money without capability" gap in this source set. Data readiness work (data audits, data cleaning, master data, data-protection-compliant pipelines) is the most defensible entry offer for a small Kenyan firm.

### Gaps
- No Accenture data-readiness percentage for Africa or Kenya was found.
- The Accenture workforce/reskilling statistics in *Work, workforce, workers* ([PDF](https://www.accenture.com/content/dam/accenture/final/accenture-com/document-2/Accenture-Work-Can-Become-Era-Generative-AI.pdf)) were not extracted.

## 5. Who captures the AI services money (Accenture investor data)

### Takeaway
The largest integrators already book billions of dollars a year in GenAI work. Accenture alone booked $5.9B in GenAI in FY2025 and earned $2.7B in GenAI/agentic revenue. Enterprise-tier implementation money goes to global SIs. Smaller firms capture it as subcontractors or through local delivery, localisation and the SME/mid-market segment.

### Cited Findings
- **[A] FY2025 bookings and revenue.** Accenture reported "generative AI new bookings of $1.8 billion for the fourth quarter and $5.9 billion for the full fiscal year 2025." In FY2025 it "tripled its revenue from generative AI and agentic AI to $2.7 billion, and nearly doubled its generative AI bookings to $5.9 billion." *Accenture Reports Fourth-Quarter and Full-Year Fiscal 2025 Results* (Sept 25, 2025; date not confirmed from masthead) — [Accenture newsroom PDF](https://newsroom.accenture.com/content/4q-full-fy25-earnings/accenture-reports-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-fiscal-2025-results.pdf); [SEC 8-K exhibit](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1467373/000146737325000213/q4fy25earnings8-kexhibit.htm); [Q4 FY25 call transcript](https://investor.accenture.com/~/media/Files/A/accenture-v4/investors/earnings-reports/2025/accenture-fourth-quarter-fiscal-2025-conference-call-transcript-updated-10-8-2025.pdf). *Meaning:* GenAI services are a multi-billion-dollar line at a single firm. For comparison, and **from background knowledge that was not re-verified this session**, Accenture's total FY2025 revenue was about $70B, so GenAI is still under 5% of its revenue.
- **[A] Africa sovereign AI cloud.** Accenture and Cassava Technologies announced a partnership (Sept 2025, per the search extract) to accelerate sovereign AI cloud adoption across Africa, starting in South Africa and later expanding to Egypt, Kenya, Morocco and Nigeria. — [Accenture newsroom](https://newsroom.accenture.com/news/2025/cassava-technologies-and-accenture-accelerate-sovereign-ai-cloud-adoption-across-africa). *Meaning:* sovereign/local AI compute is being built with Kenya on the roadmap. Local firms could provide onboarding, migration, data-residency compliance and managed services on top of it. **Deal value not found.**

### Inferences
- The global SIs capture enterprise implementation work. The uncaptured layer is mid-market and SME clients in Kenya and East Africa, who cannot afford Accenture or Deloitte rates but face the same data-protection, cyber and tax-compliance obligations.

### Gaps
- Accenture's FY2025 results for Growth Markets/EMEA covering Africa, and its managed services vs consulting split, could not be extracted because fetching was blocked.
- FY2024 GenAI bookings (believed to be about $3B, from background knowledge) were not verified this session.

## 6. Africa and Kenya: macro context, tax/regulation, and sector outlooks (Deloitte and Accenture)

### Takeaway
Kenya is a relatively fast-growing market. Deloitte projects 5.3% GDP growth in 2025 and 5.8% a year for 2025–2029 [F], with the IT sector around 9.24% of GDP. The Finance Act 2025 focuses on tighter tax administration rather than new taxes, which feeds demand for tax/e-invoicing compliance services. Deloitte and Accenture Africa-specific money-flow numbers are thin, so this remains a gap.

### Cited Findings
- **[F] Kenya growth.** Kenya's "GDP projected to grow 5.3% in 2025 from 4.7% in 2024", outpacing a forecast Sub-Saharan Africa average of 3.7%, and expected to grow "5.8% per year between 2025 and 2029." Drivers named: agriculture, government infrastructure investment, easing interest rates, regional trade. Deloitte, *East Africa economic outlook 2025* (Deloitte Insights; URL suggests July 2025) — [Deloitte Insights](https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/insights/topics/economy/emea/africa-economic-outlook-07-2025.html).
  - **Discrepancy:** Kenya's National Treasury *2025 Budget Policy Statement* puts 2024 growth at **4.6%**, while Deloitte says **4.7%** — [Treasury BPS 2025](https://www.treasury.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/2025-Budget-Policy-Statement...pdf). The difference is likely a data-vintage revision.
- **[A/claim] IT sector share.** Mobile money has "fueled significant growth in the IT sector, which directly contributes around 9.24% of the country's GDP." The same Deloitte report also says government plans to raise manufacturing to 12% of GDP in 2025 from 8% in 2024 [F/target]. — [Deloitte Insights East Africa economic outlook 2025](https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/insights/topics/economy/emea/africa-economic-outlook-07-2025.html). **Flag:** 9.24% is Deloitte's figure. - **[A] Kenya Finance Act 2025 (context).** The Finance Bill 2025 targeted about KES 30 billion in extra revenue "through measures that seek to enhance tax administration, as opposed to introducing new taxes." Assented June 2025, mostly effective 1 July 2025. **Source note:** these details come from non-Deloitte advisers ([KPMG East Africa](https://kpmg.com/ke/en/home/insights/2025/07/kenya_finance_act_2025.html); [RSM Kenya](https://www.rsm.global/kenya/news/highlights-kenya-finance-act-2025); [PKF](https://www.pkfea.com/publications/2025/kenya-budget-review-20252026-fiscal-year/)). The same is likely true in Kenya (inference).
- **[Report exists, stats not retrieved] Africa AI value chains.** Accenture, *Leapfrogging Development: Unlocking Africa's Potential in Global Value Chains with Artificial Intelligence* — [PDF](https://www.accenture.com/content/dam/accenture/final/accenture-com/document-4/Leapfrogging-Development-Unlocking-Africas-Potential-in-Global-Value-Chains-with-Artificial-Intelligence.pdf). ## 7. Where the firms disagree or diverge

### Takeaway
The firms agree on direction (AI budgets rising, readiness low) but differ in tone and in what they call the main bottleneck. Deloitte leads with regulation, risk and governance. Accenture leads with data readiness and a "reinvention-ready" digital core.

### Cited Findings
- Deloitte Q4 2024: **regulation/risk** is the top barrier (compliance concern 28% → 38%) — [Deloitte](https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/about/press-room/state-of-generative-ai.html). Accenture: **data readiness** is the binding constraint (only 7% data-ready) — [Accenture](https://www.accenture.com/en/insights/ai-data/ai-ready-data).
- Returns: Accenture reports "93% of executives report that gen AI investments are outperforming other investments" (Jan 2024) — [Accenture](https://www.accenture.com/content/dam/accenture/final/accenture-com/document-2/Accenture-reinvention-in-the-age-of-generative-AI-executive-summary.pdf). Deloitte is more cautious: over two-thirds expect 30% or fewer experiments to scale within 3–6 months — [Deloitte Q4 PDF](https://www.deloitte.com/content/dam/assets-zone3/us/en/docs/services/consulting/2024/us-state-of-gen-ai-q4.pdf). These measure different things (perceived ROI vs scaling rate), but the sentiment gap is real.
- Investment intent is consistent across firms: 78% (Deloitte, increase AI spend) and 86% (Accenture Pulse, increase GenAI investment) — [Deloitte](https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/about/press-room/state-of-generative-ai.html); [Accenture](https://www.accenture.com/us-en/insights/pulse-of-change).
- Both firms sell the services they recommend. Their surveys identify bottlenecks (governance, data, cyber) that match their own service lines, so treat the "problem" framing as partly commercial.

### Inferences
- Opportunity map for a small Kenyan company, ranked by strength of evidence:
  1. Data readiness and data-protection-compliant data pipelines (Accenture 7%; Deloitte 75% raising data-management spend).
  2. Cyber managed services and security-by-design attached to digital projects (Accenture 90% / 77% / 28%; Deloitte 58%; Deloitte East Africa banking).
  3. AI governance and compliance documentation (Deloitte 38% compliance concern, 21% mature agent governance).
  4. AI implementation and agent customisation as a subcontractor to global SIs, or directly for mid-market clients (Accenture $5.9B bookings; Deloitte 85% customising agents).
  5. AI/cloud cost optimisation (Deloitte Tech Trends 2026).
  6. Tax and e-compliance automation for SMEs (Finance Act 2025 administration push; non-Deloitte sources).

### Gaps
