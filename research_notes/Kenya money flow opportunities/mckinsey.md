## Q1. AI, compute and agentic AI: how much is being spent, and where are the bottlenecks (data readiness, governance, talent, power)?

### Takeaway
McKinsey sizes the global compute build-out at **~$6.7T of data-center capex by 2030**, with **$5.2T of it for AI**. AI equity investment was **$124.3B in 2024**. Enterprise adoption, however, is far ahead of value capture: **62% are at least experimenting with agents, but only 39% report any enterprise-level EBIT impact from AI.** Governance maturity is low: **only about one-third of organisations are at maturity level 3+ in governance or agentic-AI governance.** The money is going into infrastructure and experimentation. The unmet need is implementation, workflow redesign, governance and trust, and power. Africa has its own smaller version of this: **$10–20B in data-center investment for 1.5–2.2 GW by 2030.**

### Inferences
- The value chain runs capex (compute, power), then adoption (62% experimenting), then a value gap (only 39% with EBIT impact), then governance and trust deficits (only ~1/3 at maturity 3+). The unsolved problems McKinsey names are scaling from pilots, workflow redesign, governance of agents, and power. Each one is a services market that Kenyan firms can address remotely (governance, MLOps, data engineering) or locally (power and facilities for data centers).
- The Africa data-center pool ($20–30B revenue by 2030) is small next to the global $6.7T, but it is local, capex-heavy and power-constrained. That favours energy-plus-digital hybrid plays.

## Q2. Payments, fintech and banking revenue pools, especially in Africa

### Takeaway
Global payments revenue is **$2.5T, forecast to reach ~$3.0T by 2029 (4% CAGR)**. In Africa, McKinsey projects financial-services revenue growth of **nearly 10% a year from 2023 to 2028**, and fintech revenues **potentially reaching ~$47B by 2028 from ~$10B in 2023**. Kenya is McKinsey's reference benchmark for fintech penetration. The bottlenecks McKinsey names are the pivot from growth-at-all-costs to profitability, cybercrime, and fragmented payment rails.

### Cited Findings
9. **[FACT and FORECAST] Global payments revenue of $2.5T, reaching $3.0T by 2029.** Firm: McKinsey (Financial Services). Report: *The 2025 McKinsey Global Payments Report: Competing systems, contested outcomes*. Date: 2025 *(month unverified, typically September)*. Statistic: "$2.5 trillion in revenue from $2.0 quadrillion in value flows, supported by 3.6 trillion transactions". Revenue growth is expected "at 4 percent annually through 2029 … reaching $3.0 trillion by 2029". The range is 3% (disruption) to 6% (accelerated productivity). Themes include digital rails, digital assets and AI. Commercial meaning: this is a mature, slow-growing global pool. Growth is shifting to new rails (instant payments, stablecoins or digital assets) and AI-driven operations. — [McKinsey](https://www.mckinsey.com/industries/financial-services/our-insights/global-payments-report)
   - The 2024 edition, *Global payments in 2024: Simpler interfaces, complex reality*, exists. Its numbers were not retrieved. — [2024 report](https://www.mckinsey.com/industries/financial-services/our-insights/global-payments-in-2024-simpler-interfaces-complex-reality)

14. **[FACT, statistic questionable] Cybercrime cost in Africa.** *Redefining success* (finding 10), as rendered in the snippet: "the financial impact of cybercrime in the region was more than $4 billion—about 10 percent of Africa's total GDP." **This is internally inconsistent.** $4B is about 0.2% of a ~$2.5T African GDP (see finding 16), not 10%. ### Inferences
- Revenue pools in African fintech are growing roughly 10% a year for financial services overall, and faster for fintech. The report-level bottlenecks are profitability, fraud and cyber, and low take rates on mobile money. The second-order opportunities are RegTech, fraud and identity, SME credit, merchant tools, and cyber services for financial institutions.
- Kenya's leadership in penetration means its growth headroom is lower than Nigeria's or Egypt's. Kenya's advantage is therefore in exporting products and services, and in B2B, more than in domestic consumer growth.

## Q3. McKinsey Africa reports: growth, consumers, energy, agriculture, carbon and the green economy

### Takeaway
McKinsey's structural Africa numbers come mostly from **2023 and earlier** (MGI *Reimagining economic growth*, June 2023). Headline pools are **$3T of consumer spending from 250M new consumers by 2030**, **$1.4T from services productivity**, **345 companies with over $1B in revenue**, **agri-food over $1T by 2030**, carbon-credit technical potential of **~2,400 MtCO2e a year by 2030 against 25 Mt retired in 2022**, and **~$2T of green-manufacturing investment**. The bottlenecks named are low productivity, low smallholder returns on inputs, weak carbon-market supply, and **~600M people without electricity**.

## Q4. Energy (Global Energy Perspective) and supply-chain risk

### Takeaway
GEP 2025 forecasts electricity demand growth of **~3% a year to 2035**, driven by data centers (**+17% a year** in power demand from 2022 to 2030). Grids, storage and flexible capacity are the named investment needs. McKinsey's 2025 supply-chain pulse finds **82% affected by new tariffs**, most firms seeing risk only **to tier one**, **shrinking buffers**, and a **slowdown in advanced digitisation**.

### Cited Findings
21. **[FORECAST] Electricity demand and grid investment.** Report: *Global Energy Perspective 2025* (October 2025). Statistics: "sustained electricity demand growth at nearly 3 percent per year through 2035". "Investments in grid infrastructure, flexible capacity, energy security, and storage solutions [are] vital to accommodate rising power demand." McKinsey presents three scenarios (Continued Momentum, Sustainable Transformation, and a third). Commercial meaning: grid, storage and flexibility services are a durable spending line. — [GEP 2025](https://www.mckinsey.com/industries/energy-and-materials/our-insights/global-energy-perspective)

## Q5. Cybersecurity, SME digitisation, workforce reskilling, and AI search / generative engine optimisation (GEO)

### Takeaway
McKinsey sizes cybersecurity's addressable market at **$1.5–2.0T against a ~$150B vended market (2021), rising to just under $250B in 2024**. That is about 10% penetration, with midmarket managed services the underserved segment. AI search is becoming "the new front door to the internet": **44% of AI-search users call it their primary source**, **$750B of US revenue will funnel through AI search by 2028**, and unprepared brands face a **20–50% traffic decline**.
