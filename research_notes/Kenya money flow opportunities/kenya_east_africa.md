## Q1. Compliance-driven spend in Kenya (eTIMS, ODPC, Cybercrimes, CBK/DCP/VASP, AI strategy, SHIF/SHA, EPRA net metering)

### Takeaway
[KENYA] Kenya is stacking several new mandatory compliance rules at once:
- **eTIMS e-invoicing.** From Year of Income 2026, an expense without an eTIMS invoice is effectively added back to taxable income. Onboarding went from about 500k taxpayers (Oct 2025) to a reported 750,915 (June 2026), against a target of 1 million. That leaves a large long tail still to onboard and integrate.
- **ODPC enforcement.** It is accelerating: 96 determinations and more than KES 26m in fines in 2025.
- **Other new licensing and payroll regimes.** CBK digital-credit licensing (227 DCPs), the VASP Act 2025 (in force, but no licences yet), the SHIF 2.75% payroll levy, and the 2024 net-metering rules each create recurring spend on compliance software and services.

### Cited Findings
**eTIMS / KRA**
- Rule from the 2026 year of income: "all income and expenses declared must be fully supported by valid electronic tax invoices generated and transmitted through eTIMS or TIMS". This rests on Section 23A of the Tax Procedures Act (Cap 469B) and the Tax Procedures (Electronic Tax Invoice) Regulations, 2024. Commercial meaning: an unsupported supplier cost becomes taxable income for the buyer, so buyers will push their suppliers to issue eTIMS invoices. (S) — [The Eastleigh Voice](https://eastleighvoice.co.ke/business/364366/kra-allows-temporary-window-to-declare-expenses-without-etims-invoices-for-2025-tax-returns); KRA public notice "Validation of Income and Expenses in the Income Tax Returns" (P, not fetched because the domain was blocked): [KRA](https://www.kra.go.ke/news-center/public-notices/2323-validation-of-income-and-expenses-in-the-income-tax-returns)
- Transitional relief for 2025 returns (deadline 30 June 2026): genuine expenses without eTIMS/TIMS invoices could be claimed, subject to later verification. This is a one-year grace period. Heavy verification and audit exposure is likely from late 2026. **SHIF / SHA**
- SHIF replaced NHIF under the Social Health Authority. The contribution is 2.75% of gross salary for employees, with a KES 300/month minimum and no cap. Employers must remit by the 9th of the following month. Non-salaried people pay 2.75% of household income. (S) — [PwC Worldwide Tax Summaries](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/kenya/individual/other-taxes); [EY](https://www.ey.com/en_gl/technical/tax-alerts/kenya-employers-to-begin-making-contributions-to-social-health-insurance-fund)
- NSSF: from 1 Feb 2025 the maximum employee deduction is KES 4,320, matched by the employer. (S) — [Bridge Talent](https://bridgetalentgroup.com/nssf-shif-and-paye-compliance-in-kenya-2025-update/)

**EPRA net metering**
- Energy (Net-Metering) Regulations, 2024: domestic systems are capped at 10 kW and C&I systems at 1 MW. Customers get an export credit equal to 50% of exported energy. Amendments backdated to 1 July 2025 define "dumping", meaning unapproved export to the grid, and bill it at the base tariff. pv magazine reported this on 22 Sept 2026. (S) — [pv magazine](http://www.pv-magazine.com/2026/09/22/kenya-backdates-solar-export-charges-by-over-a-year/); [IndexBox](https://www.indexbox.io/blog/kenya-codifies-retroactive-charges-for-unauthorized-solar-exports-and-confirms-net-metering-credits/); [EPRA draft regs 2024](https://www.epra.go.ke/sites/default/files/Documents/DRAFT-NET-METERING-REGULATIONS-2024.docx)

### Inferences
- **eTIMS.** The "expense disallowance" mechanism turns e-invoicing from a VAT-filer issue into a problem for every business's supply chain. Corporates will need their small suppliers (transport, casual labour, jua kali, landlords) to issue eTIMS invoices. That creates recurring demand for:
  - eTIMS-integrated POS/ERP connectors;
  - supplier-onboarding services;
  - reconciliation to fix return rejections;
  - bookkeeping for MSMEs.
  About 250k+ of the 1 million onboarding target was still unmet as of June 2026. The pool of MSMEs that are not yet onboarded is much larger.
- **ODPC.** Fines are escalating: KES 9.4m across three notices in 2023, then more than KES 26m in 2025, plus referrals to prosecute directors. Together with mandatory registration, this supports recurring demand for DPO-as-a-service, DPIAs, consent management and breach-response retainers. The most exposed sectors, based on the entities the ODPC has fined, are digital lenders, schools, hospitality, real estate and marketing.
- **Regulated licensing.** The VASP Act and the 227 licensed DCPs form a regulated population that needs AML/KYC, cybersecurity and reporting tooling. VASP licensing will open a new compliance market once the regulations are gazetted.
- **Net metering.** The retroactive "dumping" charges create a compliance and advisory need for C&I solar owners who installed without net-metering agreements.

## Q2. Kenya's digital economy (MSMEs, mobile money, internet, data centres, BPO, remittances)

### Takeaway
[KENYA] Kenya's digital base is large, but a few trends need care:
- **Connectivity.** There are 88m mobile subscriptions (165% penetration, June 2026) and 58.5m data subscriptions (June 2025).
- **Mobile money.** There are 51.4m mobile money subscriptions and about 500k agents. Transaction volume fell about 30% in 2025, but the average ticket size rose.
- **Remittances.** They hit a record US$5.04bn in 2025, but growth was the slowest in about 15 years.
- **Data centres.** Hyperscale plans are power-constrained. The US$1bn Microsoft/G42 Olkaria project was suspended in May 2026, while smaller colocation (iXAfrica) is going ahead.

### Cited Findings
- Mobile subscriptions reached 88.0m by 30 June 2026, a penetration of 165.0%. (S, citing CA data) — [HapaKenya, 21 Sep 2026](https://hapakenya.com/2026/09/21/kenyas-mobile-subscriptions-hit-88-million-as-5g-fiber-satellite-boom/)
- Data/internet subscriptions stood at 58.5m by 30 June 2025, of which 78.2% were mobile broadband. 4G made up 81.2% of broadband subscriptions. (P) — [CA Q4 FY2024/25 Sector Statistics Report](https://www.ca.go.ke/sites/default/files/2025-09/Sector%20Statistics%20Report%20Q4%202024-2025_1.pdf)
- Mobile money subscriptions reached 51.4m by Dec 2025, about 98% penetration. Roughly 6m were added in 2025, including 2.7m in Q4. Smartphones made up 63.7% of connected devices. (S) — [Tech-ish, 13 Apr 2026](https://tech-ish.com/2026/04/13/kenya-digital-economy-2025-numbers-ca-data/); [CA](https://www.ca.go.ke/mobile-internet-and-tech-services-surge-kenya-digital-shift-accelerates)
- Mobile money transaction volumes fell about 30% in 2025 while accounts, agents and average ticket size rose. Mobile money agents increased 26.8% to 501,399. Agent deposits fell 10.1% to KES 5.5trn. (S/U) Sources conflict on the agent count: 501,399 in one, "473,000 agents and 89.5m accounts" in another. — [The Kenya Times](https://thekenyatimes.com/banking/m-pesa-traffic-slows-as-mobile-money-transactions-drop-30/); [TechTrends KE, 19 Sep 2026](https://techtrendske.co.ke/2026/09/19/kenya-mobile-money-agents-subscriptions-rise/)
- Mobile money agents transacted the equivalent of 53% of GDP in 2024 (CBK). (S) — [BitKE, Mar 2025](https://bitcoinke.io/2025/03/mobile-money-agents-in-kenya-in-2024/)
- Diaspora remittances reached US$5.04bn (KES 649.5bn) in 2025, up 1.9%. This was the first year above US$5bn and the slowest growth since the aftermath of 2008. The US supplied US$2.73bn (54.2%). (S) — [Business Daily](https://www.businessdailyafrica.com/bd/markets/currencies/diaspora-remittances-record-softest-growth-in-15-years-5331714); [Kenyan Wall Street](https://kenyanwallstreet.com/kenya-diaspora-remittances-top-us-5-billion-for-first-time-in-2025); primary data series at [CBK Diaspora Remittances](https://www.centralbank.go.ke/diaspora-remittances/)
- The CBK projected remittances of about KES 676bn in 2026. (S) — [Business Daily](https://www.businessdailyafrica.com/bd/markets/currencies/cbk-sees-diaspora-remittances-reaching-sh676bn-in-2026-5360280)
- Microsoft/G42 announced a US$1bn geothermal data centre at KenGen's Green Energy Park, Olkaria, with 100 MW in phase 1. It was suspended or stalled in May 2026 because of power and transmission capacity. The load is described as about one-third of Kenya's roughly 3,000 MW installed capacity. (S) — [DCD](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/microsoft-and-g42-data-center-in-kenya-stalled-due-to-lack-of-power-capacity/); [Citizen Digital](https://citizen.digital/article/kenyas-1b-microsoft-data-centre-project-in-limbo-over-power-concerns-n383281); [Tech-ish, 9 May 2026](https://tech-ish.com/2026/05/09/kenya-microsoft-g42-data-centre-stalled/)

A Greek firm has proposed a US$1.5bn off-grid AI data centre in Mombasa. (U, secondary) — [Lineserve](https://www.lineserve.net/blog/datacenters-in-kenya); [iAfrica](https://iafrica.com/greek-firm-proposes-1-5bn-off-grid-ai-data-centre-in-mombasa-sidestepping-the-constraint-that-stalled-olkaria/)

### Inferences
- **Remittances.** Growth is flattening, so the opportunity is less about new volume and more about lower cost and value-add: diaspora investment, property and bill-pay, and compliance products.
- **Mobile money.** Larger, fewer transactions suggest merchant and business payments are replacing P2P micro-transfers. That favours B2B reconciliation, eTIMS-linked till and paybill integration, and SME finance tooling.
- **Data centres.** The power constraint is the real bottleneck. Opportunities sit in captive power, energy management and backup for colocation and edge facilities rather than hyperscale.

## Q3. Kenya energy and carbon (C&I solar, tariffs, outages, carbon markets)

### Takeaway
[KENYA] Captive C&I solar dominates Kenya's small installed solar base: 300.5 MW C&I captive out of 514.1 MW total solar by June 2025. Net-metering rules now cap C&I at 1 MW and penalise unauthorised export. The Carbon Markets Regulations 2024 require projects to share benefits: 40% of profits after costs for land-based projects and 25% for other projects. A 2026 rule book adds a national registry and a 10 Mt carbon budget to 2030.

### Cited Findings
- Installed solar reached 514.1 MW by June 2025, of which C&I captive solar was 300.5 MW. (U/S) — [SurgePV](https://www.surgepv.com/blog/kenya-solar-regulations-net-metering); [Pulse Kenya, Sept 2026](https://www.pulse.co.ke/story/epra-tightens-solar-export-rules-inside-kenyas-net-metering-system-2026092520081554519)
- Net metering is capped at 1 MW for C&I and 10 kW for domestic, with a 50% export credit. Backdated "dumping" charges apply from 1 Jul 2025. (S) — [pv magazine, 22 Sep 2026](http://www.pv-magazine.com/2026/09/22/kenya-backdates-solar-export-charges-by-over-a-year/)
- The Climate Change (Carbon Markets) Regulations, 2024 (LN 84 of 2024) came into force on 17 May 2024. Regulation 29 requires land-based projects to share at least 40% of profits after costs with communities, and non-land-based projects at least 25%. (P) — [Kenya Law](https://new.kenyalaw.org/akn/ke/act/ln/2024/84/eng@2024-06-07); [Oraro & Co](https://www.oraro.co.ke/the-climate-change-carbon-markets-regulations-2024/); [EY](https://www.ey.com/en_gl/technical/tax-alerts/kenya-publishes-climate-change--carbon-markets--regulations--202)
- A new carbon-market rule book (Aug 2026) puts the Kenya National Carbon Registry into operation. It sets a 10-million-tonne carbon budget before 2030 and a three-stage approval process, with the stated aims of preventing double counting and improving benefit-sharing. (S) — [Big3Africa, 8 Aug 2026](https://big3africa.org/2026/08/08/kenya-unveils-new-carbon-market-rules/); [DevelopmentAid](https://www.developmentaid.org/news-stream/post/209853/kenyas-carbon-registry)

### Inferences
- **Solar.** Captive C&I solar is the growth segment. The 1 MW net-metering cap and the anti-dumping rules push larger sites towards batteries, export limiters, energy management and PPA/leasing structures. These are recurring O&M and compliance revenue lines.
- **Carbon.** The registry, the benefit-sharing audits and the carbon budget create recurring MRV, registry-filing and community benefit-distribution admin work. Mobile-money payout rails fit the benefit-distribution piece.

### Gaps
- Current KPLC tariff levels (KES/kWh by band after the 2023 review) were not retrieved.
- Outage statistics were not retrieved. The Climate Change (Amendment) Act 2023 wording was not fetched.

## Q4. Kenya agriculture exports, EUDR and CBAM

### Takeaway
[KENYA] EUDR is postponed to 30 Dec 2026 for large operators and 30 June 2027 for micro/small operators, and more than 55% of Kenyan coffee goes to the EU. Every exporting cooperative therefore faces a mandatory geolocation and traceability requirement in the next 6–9 months. Horticulture earnings are volatile: Q1 2025 fell 11%, while H1 2025 rose 20% in KES terms.

### Cited Findings
- EUDR application dates: 30 Dec 2026 for large operators and traders, and 30 Jun 2027 for micro and small operators, postponed from Dec 2025. More than 55% of Kenyan coffee exports go to the EU. Cut-off: no deforestation after 31 Dec 2020. (S) — [Agrosocial Services](https://agrosocialservices.co.ke/certification-compliance/eudr-compliance-kenya-coffee-exporters-2026-deadline/); [The Standard](https://www.standardmedia.co.ke/business/article/2001525328/new-eu-laws-brew-trouble-for-kenya-s-smallholder-coffee-farmers); [News Ghana](https://www.newsghana.com.gh/kenyas-coffee-farmers-race-to-meet-eu-digital-compliance-deadline/)
- Horticulture exports were KES 87.3bn in H1 2025, up 20% from KES 73.3bn in H1 2024. (S) — [Nairobi Business Monthly](https://nairobibusinessmonthly.com/horticulture-exports-surge-as-kenya-earns-more-from-global-markets/)
- Q1 2025 horticulture earnings were about US$309.4m, down 11.2% from US$349m. (S) — [Xinhua, 14 Jun 2025](https://english.news.cn/africa/20250614/7ec6ad291f5a4f7cb5ae49563ecf476a/c.html); [Capital FM](https://capitalfm.africa/kenyas-horticulture-exports-down-11pc-in-q1-2025/)
- Q3 2025 horticulture exports were about US$195.6m, with the Netherlands the leading market. (S) — [FloralDaily](https://www.floraldaily.com/article/9801881/kenyan-horticultural-exports-led-by-the-netherlands-in-q3-2025/)

### Inferences
- **EUDR.** Traceability for smallholders means polygon mapping, due-diligence statements and chain-of-custody data. It is a mandatory, recurring (per-shipment) service for coffee cooperatives and exporters. Tea exposure depends on whether it is in scope. Tea is *not* an EUDR commodity; coffee, cocoa, palm, soy, cattle, rubber and wood are. That is from memory and should be verified before any tea claims.
- **Horticulture.** Volatility in earnings makes cold-chain and certification (GlobalG.A.P., MRL testing) the steady spend.

### Gaps
- There is no current KNBS/AFA annual total for horticulture in 2025.
- I found no Kenya-specific EU CBAM exposure data. CBAM covers steel, aluminium, cement, fertiliser, hydrogen and electricity, so Kenya's direct exposure is likely small. That is an inference only.

## Q5. [EAST AFRICA] Regional exportability: e-invoicing and data protection in Uganda, Tanzania and Rwanda

### Takeaway
[AFRICA / EAST AFRICA] All four EAC neighbours have the same two compliance building blocks: mandatory fiscal e-invoicing and data-protection registration with fines. The systems are not interoperable: Uganda EFRIS, Kenya eTIMS/TIMS, Tanzania EFD/VFD and Rwanda EBM "do not talk to each other". A Kenyan compliance product could therefore be exported through country-specific connectors.

### Cited Findings
- **Uganda EFRIS.** From 1 July 2025, businesses in 12 designated sectors (including manufacturing, mining, construction, transport, and ICT) must issue EFRIS e-invoices and receipts. This is on top of the existing obligation for all VAT-registered taxpayers. Businesses with turnover under UGX 10m are exempt. (P/S) — [URA](https://ura.go.ug/en/additional-taxpayers-required-to-use-efris/); [URA EFRIS](https://ura.go.ug/en/efris/); [SM&Co Advocates, 1 Sep 2025](https://smco.ug/2025/09/01/efris-expansion-to-new-business-sectors-what-taxpayers-need-to-know/)
- Uganda EFRIS penalties are being tightened. (S) — [Daily Monitor](https://www.monitor.co.ug/uganda/business/prosper/businesses-face-tougher-penalties-over-efris-5438906)
- **Tanzania** e-invoicing and e-reporting (EFD/VFD) is covered in the VATupdate country booklet (Jul 2026). (S) — [VATupdate](https://www.vatupdate.com/2026/07/07/tanzania-e-invoicing-e-reporting-country-booklet/); [VATupdate Africa overview, May 2025](https://www.vatupdate.com/2025/05/17/current-status-of-e-invoicing-in-africa/)
- **Tanzania PDPC.** Registration is mandatory under the PDPA 2022, with a final deadline of 8 April 2026. After that, unregistered entities face enforcement. Failing to register carries a fine of TZS 100,000–5m and/or up to 5 years' imprisonment. (P/S) — [PDPC](https://www.pdpc.go.tz/core/en/minister-kairuki-issues-final-three-month-ultimatum-on-personal-data-registration2026-04-01-222818/); [The Chanzo, 27 Mar 2026](https://thechanzo.com/2026/03/27/data-protection-commission-issues-final-warning-ahead-of-april-enforcement-deadline); [DLA Piper Africa](https://www.dlapiperafrica.com/en/tanzania/insights/2025/legal-article-a28156-registration-with-the-ersonal-data-protection-commission-in-tanzania-flyer-v2)
- **Uganda PDPO.** Registration of controllers, processors and collectors is mandatory (s.29 DPPA 2019) and must be renewed annually. In 2025 the PDPO found Google in breach and ordered it to register locally, and it issued guidance on offshore entities. (S) — [CIPESA, Jul 2025](https://cipesa.org/2025/07/ugandan-regulator-finds-google-in-breach-of-countrys-data-protection-law-orders-local-registration/); [DLA Piper Privacy Matters, Aug 2025](https://privacymatters.dlapiper.com/2025/08/uganda-data-protection-regulator-clarifies-compliance-requirements-for-offshore-entities/)
- **Rwanda.** Law No. 058/2021 has been fully enforceable since 15 Oct 2023. Controllers and processors must register with NCSA; registration is free and online. Operating without a certificate carries a fine of RWF 2–5m or 1% of prior-year turnover. (P/S) — [Rwanda DPO (NCSA)](https://dpo.gov.rw/); [Dark Reading](https://www.darkreading.com/cyber-risk/navigating-rwanda-new-data-protection-law)

### Inferences
- The EAC compliance stack is converging but fragmented. A "one dashboard, four tax authorities" e-invoicing and data-protection compliance service for regional SMEs and multinationals is a plausible export product from a Nairobi base.
- Tanzania's April 2026 deadline has just passed, so enforcement-driven demand is likely in H2 2026.

## Q6. [AFRICA] Continental money flows: VC funding, AfCFTA, PAPSS

### Takeaway
[AFRICA] African tech funding recovered in 2025: US$4.1bn per Partech (including debt) and US$3.2bn per Africa: The Big Deal. Kenya ranked #1 on both: US$1.04bn (+72%) per Partech and US$984m (32% share) per The Big Deal. Debt made up 48–60% of Kenya's funding, driven by energy and asset finance. This is "boring money" chasing infrastructure-type cash flows. PAPSS now connects 19 countries and more than 150 banks, with KCB live since Feb 2025 and Pesalink linked in Feb 2026.

### Cited Findings
- **Partech 2025 Africa Tech VC Report** (published 22 Jan 2026): African tech funding rebounded to US$4.1bn, driven by record debt. Details:
  - Kenya was #1 with US$1.04bn (+72% YoY), 48% of it debt, and 91 deals (+10%).
  - Kenya closed 4 of Africa's 9 megadeals, and its debt volume was more than twice that of Egypt.
  - Kenya, South Africa, Egypt and Nigeria together took more than 70% of capital.
  (P) — [Partech press release](https://partechpartners.com/news/2025-partech-africa-tech-vc-report-african-tech-funding-rebounds-to-us41b-driven-by-record-debt-activity-and-disciplined-equity-growth); [Report PDF](https://partech-admin.prod.unomena.io/media/documents/2025_Partech-Africa-Tech-VC-Report.pdf)
- **Africa: The Big Deal (2025)**: Africa raised US$3.2bn. Details:
  - Kenya raised US$984m (32%), up 52% from US$638m in 2024, the highest since 2022.
  - Kenya's split was US$582m debt (60%) and US$383m equity, with energy megadeals leading.
  - The number of Kenyan ventures raising at least US$100k fell 23% to 75.
  (S, reporting ATBD data) — [The EastAfrican](https://www.theeastafrican.co.ke/tea/business-tech/kenya-takes-largest-share-of-africa-venture-capital-funding-5329968); [Tech-ish, 16 Jan 2026](https://tech-ish.com/2026/01/16/kenya-africa-startup-funding-2025/)

KCB (Nairobi, 27 Feb 2025) and Bank of Kigali (26 Feb 2025) were the first banks in their countries to go live. Pesalink partnered with PAPSS on 26 Feb 2026. - Kenya's funding lead is debt-led and concentrated in a few deals, while the number of early-stage rounds is falling. That supports a "boring money" thesis built on compliance-plus-infrastructure services.
- PAPSS and Pesalink could lower the cost of intra-EAC B2B payments, which would support cross-border compliance-SaaS billing and AfCFTA trade.
