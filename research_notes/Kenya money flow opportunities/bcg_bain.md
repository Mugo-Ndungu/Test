## 1. AI value, spending and who captures it (BCG)

### Takeaway
BCG's surveys show corporate AI budgets roughly doubling (0.8% to about 1.7% of revenue in 2026). They also show value concentrated in a small elite: 4–5% of firms get substantial value, and 60% get little or none. So the money is flowing, but most buyers cannot turn it into results. The gap sits in workflow redesign, training, data and agent integration, not in model access. For a small Kenyan firm, the opening is to sell implementation, training and "make-AI-work" services, not AI itself.

### Cited Findings
- **BCG, "Where's the Value in AI?", Oct 2024 (survey of 1,000 CxOs, 59 countries). F:** only 26% of companies "have developed the necessary set of capabilities to move beyond proofs of concept and generate tangible value", and only 4% create substantial value. Leaders generate 62% of their value in core business processes. BCG's press headline is "74% of Companies Struggle to Achieve and Scale Value". *Commercial meaning:* three-quarters of buyers are stuck at pilot stage, so there is demand for scaling and integration help. — [BCG report page](https://www.bcg.com/publications/2024/wheres-value-in-ai); [BCG press release 24 Oct 2024](https://www.bcg.com/press/24october2024-ai-adoption-in-2024-74-of-companies-struggle-to-achieve-and-scale-value); [PDF](https://web-assets.bcg.com/a5/37/be4ddf26420e95aa7107a35aae8d/bcg-wheres-the-value-in-ai.pdf)
- **BCG, "The Widening AI Value Gap: Build for the Future 2025", Sept 2025 (1,250 executives). F:** 5% of companies are "future-built" and 60% "report little to no value despite massive investment". About 35% are scaling but not yet showing significant results. Future-built firms show 1.7x revenue growth, 3.6x shareholder returns (TSR) and 2.7x ROI on AI. — [BCG press release 30 Sept 2025](https://www.bcg.com/press/30september2025-ai-leaders-outpace-laggards-revenue-growth-cost-savings); [PDF](https://media-publications.bcg.com/The-Widening-AI-Value-Gap-Sept-2025.pdf)
- **Same report. F for 2025 / FC for 2028:** agents account for 17% of total AI value in 2025, "expected to reach 29% by 2028". Future-built companies put 15% of their AI budgets into agents. *Commercial meaning:* agent build, integration and monitoring is the fastest-growing slice of AI budgets. — [BCG press release](https://www.bcg.com/press/30september2025-ai-leaders-outpace-laggards-revenue-growth-cost-savings)
- **BCG, "AI Radar 2026: As AI Investments Surge, CEOs Take the Lead", Jan 2026. FC (stated spending intent):** companies expect to double AI spending in 2026, "from 0.8% to about 1.7% of revenues". CEOs have committed "more than 30%" of this year's AI investment to agentic AI. About 90% believe agents will produce measurable ROI in 2026. 94% will keep investing at current or higher levels even if there is no payoff within the year. Half of CEOs say their job is on the line if AI does not pay off in 2026. *Commercial meaning:* budgets are locked in and under pressure to show ROI, which creates demand for ROI measurement, change management and agent operations. — [BCG AI Radar 2026](https://www.bcg.com/publications/2026/as-ai-investments-surge-ceos-take-the-lead); [BCG press release 15 Jan 2026](https://www.bcg.com/press/15january2026-as-ai-investments-surge-ceos-take-lead); [PDF](https://web-assets.bcg.com/73/8e/cc44cbc14a3b81695f8a3de28ff1/ai-radar-2026-web-jan-2026-edit.pdf)
- **BCG, "AI at Work 2025: Momentum Builds, but Gaps Remain", June 2025 (10,635 employees, 11 countries). F:** 72% of leaders, managers and frontline workers are regular GenAI users. Frontline use, however, "stalled at 51%", a "silicon ceiling". Regular use is sharply higher among employees who get at least five hours of training and in-person coaching. *Commercial meaning:* named bottleneck = training and workflow redesign, which suits a low-cost, English-speaking Kenyan training and enablement provider. — [BCG publication](https://www.bcg.com/publications/2025/ai-at-work-momentum-builds-but-gaps-remain); [BCG press release 26 Jun 2025](https://www.bcg.com/press/26june2025-beyond-ai-adoption-full-potential). The 51% and 5-hour figures were carried by [UNLEASH](https://www.unleash.ai/unleashworld/hr-tech/bcgs-ai-at-work-2025-report-four-takeaways-for-hr-leaders/) **[aggregator; consistent with BCG summary]**
- A June 2026 follow-up exists: "AI Is Reshaping Jobs Faster Than Companies Are Reshaping Work" (BCG press, 3 Jun 2026). I did not extract its figures. — [BCG press](https://www.bcg.com/press/3june2026-ai-reshaping-jobs-faster-than-companies-reshaping-work)

### Inferences
- Across three BCG surveys (2024, 2025, 2026), the value-capture rate barely moves (4%, then 5%) while spending doubles. The growing gap between spending and results is the pool of money for implementation services.
- Frontline adoption depends on training. Training, localisation and agent-supervision labour are services a Nairobi firm can deliver remotely at lower cost.

### Gaps
- BCG AI surveys cover mainly Asia, Europe and North America. I found no Africa-specific AI adoption or spending figure from BCG.
- I could not verify exhibit or page numbers because the PDFs were blocked.

## 2. AI infrastructure economics: compute, power and the revenue gap (Bain)

### Takeaway
Bain puts the scale of AI infrastructure at roughly $500B a year in capex and about 200 GW of new power by 2030. Funding it needs about $2T of annual revenue, and Bain expects a shortfall of about $800B. The named bottlenecks are power, grid, construction and revenue monetisation. Bain's enterprise view is more upbeat than BCG's: leaders report 10–25% EBITDA gains.

### Cited Findings
- **Bain, "Technology Report 2025" (6th annual), press release 23 Sept 2025. FC:** "$2 trillion in annual revenue is needed to fund computing power to meet anticipated AI demand by 2030". AI companies' revenue is likely to "fall $800 billion short". Global incremental AI compute demand "could reach 200 GW by 2030". Meeting it could need about $500B a year in capital investment. — [Bain press release](https://www.bain.com/about/media-center/press-releases/20252/$2-trillion-in-new-revenue-needed-to-fund-ais-scaling-trend---bain--companys-6th-annual-global-technology-report/); [Bain Technology Report hub](https://www.bain.com/insights/topics/technology-report/). The $500B figure is also stated by [The Consulting Report](https://www.theconsultingreport.com/bain-company-report-warns-2-trillion-revenue-gap-to-meet-ai-compute-demand-by-2030/) **[aggregator]**
- **Bain Technology Report 2025, "Building the Foundation for Agentic AI" chapter. FC:** over the next 3–5 years, "5% to 10% of technology spending" could go to foundational agent capabilities: agent platforms, communication protocols, real-time data access and agent discoverability. As much as half of overall enterprise tech spending could eventually go to AI agents running across the enterprise. — [Bain chapter](https://www.bain.com/insights/building-the-foundation-for-agentic-AI-technology-report-2025/)
- **Bain Technology Report 2025, "State of the Art of Agentic AI Transformation". F (reported outcomes):** leaders moved from pilots to profit, with "10% to 25% EBITDA gains" over the last two years from scaling AI across core workflows. — [Bain chapter](https://www.bain.com/insights/state-of-the-art-of-agentic-ai-transformation-technology-report-2025/)
- **Bain Technology Report 2025, "Sovereign Tech, Fragmented World". Qualitative:** tariffs, export controls and government pushes for sovereign AI are "accelerating the fragmentation of global technology supply chains". *Commercial meaning:* governments, including African ones, will fund local data residency and sovereign-cloud work, which creates compliance and hosting-integration niches. — [Bain chapter](https://www.bain.com/insights/sovereign-tech-fragmented-world-technology-report-2025/)
- **Bain, "AI's Trillion-Dollar Opportunity", Technology Report 2024, 25 Sept 2024. FC:** the AI products and services market will grow "40 and 55 percent annually" to reach "$780–990 billion by 2027". AI workloads could grow 25–35% a year through 2027. Data centres will scale "from today's 50–200 megawatts to more than a gigawatt". Large data centres costing $1–4B today could cost $10–25B within five years. Bain named supply-chain crunches, especially in components, as a risk. — [Bain insight](https://www.bain.com/insights/ais-trillion-dollar-opportunity-tech-report-2024/); [Bain press release](https://www.bain.com/about/media-center/press-releases/2024/market-for-ai-products-and-services-could-reach-up-to--$990-billion-by-2027-finds-bain--companys-5th-annual-global-technology-report/)
- **Bain, data centre and energy research, 2025. FC:** US data-centre electricity demand could double to 409 TWh by 2030. Hyperscalers and colocation providers will spend about $1.8T on US data-centre capex over 2024–2030. Data centres could account for 44% of US load growth over 2023–2028. The press release names "risks of power constraints and construction delays". — [Bain press release](https://www.bain.com/about/media-center/press-releases/20252/next-phase-of-data-center-growth-to-be-more-disciplined-but-risks-of-power-constraints-and-construction-delays-remain-bain--co-research/); [Bain Energy Executive Agenda 2025](https://www.bain.com/insights/energy-agenda-2025-new-challenges-new-innovations/). Some of these figures came via [Utility Dive](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/data-center-load-growth-us-electricity-bills-bain/730691/) **[aggregator; attribution to exact Bain page unverified]**

### Inferences
- Hyperscale capex is out of reach for a small Kenyan firm. The reachable second-order layers are: (a) data labelling, evaluation and agent QA labour; (b) agent "plumbing" for local businesses (APIs, data access, M-Pesa and ERP connectors); (c) the energy side of Kenyan colocation and edge sites (solar plus storage, energy management).
- Bain's $800B gap implies pricing pressure on AI vendors to find paying use cases. That favours resellers and integrators who can show quick ROI in emerging markets.

### Gaps
- Bain's 2025 report contains no Africa-specific compute or data-centre numbers that I could find.
- I downloaded the Bain 2025 PDF (via a mediapost S3 mirror) but could not parse it, so page numbers are unverified.

## 3. Payments and fintech revenue pools (BCG), global and Africa

### Takeaway
Global payments revenue is $1.9T (2024) and slowing to about 4% growth. Fintech is $504B in revenue, growing 22%. Africa is BCG's fastest-growing fintech region: about 13x growth to about $65B by 2030. BCG explicitly names the unsolved problems as B2B and government payments, credit and data-driven lending, and interoperable infrastructure, not consumer P2P wallets.

### Cited Findings
- **BCG, "Beyond Payments: Unlocking Africa's Second FinTech Wave", 2026 (launched at the Inclusive FinTech Forum, Kigali; press coverage dated May 2026). FC:** African fintech revenues are projected to grow "roughly 13x to approximately $65 billion" by 2030. Africa accounts for "roughly 74% of global mobile money transaction volume". More than half of African fintechs operate in payments and lending, and more than 60% of equity funding went to these segments. The next phase is "expanding from consumer payments to B2B/Government payments and credit", with gaps in "B2B payments and data-driven lending". The report names Kenya's PesaLink, Nigeria's NIP and agent networks as the rails of the first wave. *Commercial meaning:* the white space is B2B invoicing and collections, government-to-business and business-to-government payment integration, SME credit scoring and embedded lending infrastructure. — [BCG publication](https://www.bcg.com/publications/2026/beyond-payments-unlocking-africas-second-fintech-wave); [PDF](https://web-assets.bcg.com/pdf-src/prod-live/beyond-payments-unlocking-africas-second-fintech-wave.pdf). The $65B figure is also in [ITWeb Africa](https://itweb.africa/article/africa-fintech-revenues-to-hit-65-billion-by-2030/rW1xL75naZbMRk6m) **[aggregator]**. The base-year revenue behind "13x" (about $5B implied) was **not verified**.
- **BCG (with QED), "Global Fintech 2026: From Recovery to Resurgence", press release 1 Jun 2026. F (2025 actuals):** global fintech revenues are "$504 billion" and grew 22%, "more than four times faster than incumbent financial institutions". 74% of the largest public fintechs are profitable, with EBITDA margins up 400bp to 20%. Equity funding reached $58B (+53%). Fintech M&A rose from $105B (2023) to $184B (2024) to $251B (2025). There were 42 fintech IPOs (+50%). Fintech is about 4% of the global financial services revenue pool. — [BCG press](https://www.bcg.com/press/1june2026-global-fintech-revenues-surpass-half-trillion-dollars); [BCG report](https://www.bcg.com/publications/2026/from-recovery-to-resurgence-in-global-fintech)
- **BCG, "Fintech's Next Chapter: Scaled Winners and Emerging Disruptors" (Global Fintech Report 2025), Jun 2025.** Headline: "Profits Rise, AI Reshapes the Landscape, and Scaled Winners Come of Age". I did not extract detailed numbers. — [BCG publication](https://www.bcg.com/publications/2025/fintechs-scaled-winners-emerging-disruptors); [BCG press 2 Jun 2025](https://www.bcg.com/press/2june2025-fintechs-next-chapter)
- **BCG, "Global Payments Report 2025: The Future Is Anything but Stable", press release 22 Sept 2025. F:** payments revenue reached $1.9T in 2024. **FC:** it will reach $2.4T by 2029, with growth moderating to about 4% a year. BCG names five forces: agentic AI, stablecoins and digital currencies, fintech disruption, real-time account-to-account (A2A) payments, and cost transformation. Agentic AI is "set to influence over $1 trillion in e-commerce spending". Stablecoins handled more than $26T of volume in 2024, but "real-world payments still account for only one per cent". *Commercial meaning:* incumbents are squeezed and will outsource cost work. The new rails (A2A, stablecoin payouts, agent-initiated checkout) need compliance, KYC and integration layers. — [BCG press](https://www.bcg.com/press/22september2025-reshape-global-payments-landscape); [BCG report](https://www.bcg.com/publications/2025/global-payments-transformation-amid-instability)
- **BCG, "Unlocking the Fintech Potential in Africa", 2023 [older, foundational; flagged].** Earlier baseline for Africa fintech sizing. — [BCG](https://www.bcg.com/publications/2023/africa-unlocking-the-fintech-potential)

### Inferences
- The strongest Kenya-specific signal: BCG says wave 2 is B2B, government and credit, while more than 60% of funding is still stuck in payments and lending. Services that let SMEs and government agencies use PesaLink or M-Pesa at scale are underbuilt: reconciliation, invoicing, KYB, credit-data pipelines and collections.
- Stablecoins are only 1% "real-world payments" today (BCG). A compliant stablecoin payout and remittance layer for the Kenyan diaspora and freelancers is an early-stage bet, not proven demand.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm the Middle East & Africa share of the $2.4T payments pool or the regional breakdown in the 2026 fintech report.
- I did not verify the Kenya-specific numbers inside "Beyond Payments".

## 4. Private capital, wealth and energy/climate money (Bain PE, BCG Wealth, BCG Africa energy)

### Takeaway
Private equity (PE) is recovering but narrowly: buyouts of $904B in 2025 with $1.3T of dry powder. Wealth in the Middle East & Africa grew 12.3%, but Africa is held back by thin capital markets. BCG sizes Africa's universal energy access need at $180–200B and developing-country adaptation needs at more than $380B a year, with less than a fifth currently funded. Climate and energy are where the biggest Africa-relevant capital gaps sit.

### Cited Findings
- **Bain, "Global Private Equity Report 2026", Mar 2026. F (2025 data):** buyout value rose 44% to $904B. Exit value rose 47% to $717B. Buyout dry powder is "$1.3 trillion", with sovereign wealth a growing equity source. The recovery was "markedly narrow": 13 megadeals over $10B accounted for $274B (30%). *Commercial meaning:* PE is concentrated in megadeals, so small-cap African deals will rely on DFIs and specialists. The report stresses value creation (operational improvement), which drives demand for portfolio-company ops and data services. — [Bain press release](https://www.bain.com/about/media-center/press-releases/2026/private-equity-resurgence-gathers-steam-as-new-era-challenges-firms-to-enhance-value-creationbain--company-global-pe-report/); [Bain report hub](https://www.bain.com/insights/topics/global-private-equity-report/)
- **BCG, Global Wealth Report 2026 ("Where the Next Wave of Wealth Is Coming From"), May 2026. F:** global financial wealth rose 10.7% to $333T in 2025. In the Middle East & Africa, "nominal wealth grew 12.3%". Africa's 7% five-year CAGR is "tempered by geopolitical uncertainty, uneven inflation, and the underdeveloped capital markets". **FC:** emerging markets will add $12T of financial wealth by 2030. — [BCG GWR 2026](https://www.bcg.com/publications/2026/where-the-next-wave-of-wealth-will-emerge); [BCG GWR 2026 alt page](https://www.bcg.com/publications/2026/global-wealth-growth-in-an-era-of-reordering); [BCG GWR 2025](https://www.bcg.com/publications/2025/global-wealth-report-2025-rethinking-rules-for-growth). **Note:** the snippet's attribution of specific lines to the 2025 vs 2026 edition is ambiguous. The $333T/2025 figure most likely belongs to GWR 2026.
- **BCG, "Africa Unleashed: Powering Prosperity Through Energy Access", 2025. FC:** "$180-200 billion is required for universal energy access". Faster energy access could deliver "over $500 billion in GDP gains by 2040". *Commercial meaning:* distributed solar, mini-grids, pay-as-you-go (PAYG) collections, installer training and energy-data services. Kenya is a regional hub for all of these. — [BCG publication](https://www.bcg.com/publications/2025/powering-prosperity-through-energy-access)
- **BCG, "Actions to Mobilize Investment in Adaptation and Resilience", 2025. FC and F:** developing countries "will require more than $380 billion in annual adaptation funding by 2030". Current flows are "less than one-fifth of that and mostly from public sources". Private capital is constrained by "fragmented planning, limited project pipelines, and uncertainty over returns". *Commercial meaning:* the named bottleneck is the project pipeline and bankability, which creates demand for project preparation, MRV (measurement, reporting and verification), climate-risk data and grant/blended-finance packaging. — [BCG publication](https://www.bcg.com/publications/2025/mobilizing-investment-adaptation-resilience)
- **BCG, cybersecurity. F:** organisations spend "$200 billion a year on cybersecurity products and services" but face a "28% vacancy rate" in cyber jobs (2024 publication). BCG's 2026 piece "Cybersecurity Spending and AI Threat Trends for 2026" notes that cyber spending grew about 12% in 2025, above the 7% expected, driven by securing AI assets. That growth figure came via the search extract and is **unverified against the page**. — [BCG 2024](https://www.bcg.com/publications/2024/cybersecurity-talent-shortage-close-the-gap); [BCG 2026](https://www.bcg.com/publications/2026/cybersecurity-spending-ai-threat-trends)

### Inferences
- Energy access and adaptation are the largest Africa-specific pools BCG names, and both are pipeline-constrained rather than capital-constrained. That is exactly where small service firms fit: preparing projects, collecting data, and running PAYG and metering operations.
- A cyber talent vacancy rate of 28% combined with AI-driven spending supports a Kenyan managed security (SOC) or security-awareness services play, especially for banks and SACCOs complying with Kenya's Data Protection Act. That last link is my inference, not a BCG claim.

### Gaps
- I found no BCG report specific to African cities or consumers in 2024–2026. Bain's Africa consumer work ("Growing with Africa's consumers") appears older than 2024 **[flagged as old]**.
- Bain's Energy & Natural Resources Report: the latest hub page found is for 2023. For 2025 I found only the Energy Executive Agenda, with no Africa numbers.
- I found no BCG supply-chain reshoring figures. Not researched due to tool limits.
- I found no Bain SME/B2B payments report or Bain "e-Conomy"-style Africa report. The e-Conomy series is Google/Temasek/Bain for Southeast Asia, and I found no Africa equivalent from Bain.

## 5. AI search and distribution shift (Bain), and where BCG and Bain disagree

### Takeaway
Bain finds that AI summaries now end most searches, with 15–25% less organic traffic. That shifts marketing spend toward being cited by AI answers, which is a new services niche. On AI ROI, BCG stresses that most firms get little value, while Bain stresses leaders' EBITDA gains and a macro revenue shortfall. The two views are complementary but differ in emphasis.

### Cited Findings
- **Bain, "Goodbye Clicks, Hello AI: Zero-Click Search Redefines Marketing", Feb 2025. F (survey):** about 80% of search users rely on AI summaries at least 40% of the time. About 60% of searches now end without the user clicking through. This reduces organic web traffic by an estimated 15–25%. *Commercial meaning:* "generative engine optimisation", structured product data and AI-answer visibility services for African brands, hotels and SMEs. — [Bain insight](https://www.bain.com/insights/goodbye-clicks-hello-ai-zero-click-search-redefines-marketing/); [Bain press release](https://www.bain.com/about/media-center/press-releases/20252/consumer-reliance-on-ai-search-results-signals-new-era-of-marketing--bain--company-about-80-of-search-users-rely-on-ai-summaries-at-least-40-of-the-time-on-traditional-search-engines-about-60-of-searches-now-end-without-the-user-progressing-to-a/)
- **Disagreement or difference in emphasis on AI ROI:**
  - BCG says 60% of firms get "little to no value", and only 5% are future-built ([BCG Sept 2025](https://www.bcg.com/press/30september2025-ai-leaders-outpace-laggards-revenue-growth-cost-savings)).
  - Bain highlights leaders achieving "10% to 25% EBITDA gains" ([Bain 2025](https://www.bain.com/insights/state-of-the-art-of-agentic-ai-transformation-technology-report-2025/)). At the industry level, however, Bain warns of an "$800 billion" revenue shortfall against infrastructure needs ([Bain press](https://www.bain.com/about/media-center/press-releases/20252/$2-trillion-in-new-revenue-needed-to-fund-ais-scaling-trend---bain--companys-6th-annual-global-technology-report/)).
  - Both firms agree that value concentrates among leaders and that agents are the next budget line. BCG says more than 30% of AI budgets go to agents in 2026. Bain says 5–10% of tech spend goes to agent foundations and up to half of tech spend could eventually go to agents.
  - They differ in where the risk sits. BCG sees execution risk inside firms. Bain adds supply-side financing risk (power and capex versus revenue).
- **Agentic commerce overlap:** BCG says agentic AI will influence more than $1T of e-commerce spend ([BCG payments press](https://www.bcg.com/press/22september2025-reshape-global-payments-landscape)). This matches Bain's zero-click finding: discovery and checkout are both moving into AI agents.

### Inferences
- For a Kenyan SME, the combined signal is to sell "AI visibility plus agent-ready checkout" (product feeds, M-Pesa and card APIs exposed to agents) to local merchants and tourism operators before incumbent agencies do.
- Because the two firms agree that value concentrates, the best clients are the 60–74% of stuck adopters, who need cheap, fast implementation.

### Gaps
- I found no Africa-specific data from Bain or BCG on AI search adoption.
- The Bain PE 2026 and Tech 2025 PDFs were not parseable, so I could not extract exhibit numbers or verbatim chart labels.
